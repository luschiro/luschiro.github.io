Petrochronology Week 1

Created on March 26, 2025

2025   ·   petrochronology   ·   geology

intro

So, I created this website a while ago to track my PhD and my work as a geology researcher at Unicamp. But as life happens, I’m only updating this now. But anyway, in my first semester back in geology as a researcher and a PhD student, I had a Special Topics class, where I wrote an essay about chemical mapping; and a Remote Sensing and Geotechnologies class to get a hold of some new tools (ENVI).

But now, I have just wrapped up the first week of a short class about Petrochronology, by Prof. Vinícius T. Meira, who also happens to be my advisor. The Petrochronology class will have two weeks, and here is a summary of the amazing first week:

day 1: Intro to Petrochronology

The first day of the course was an introduction to some concepts and the main body of work on Petrochronology. A good first reading on these topics is, of course, the review volume from Kohn, Lanari and Engi (2017) - Petrochronology: Methods and Applications. It covers the main methods and their applications.

A nice quote from this work that gives a nice glimpse of what Petrochronology is all about:

…a rock does not have, sensu stricto, one age

day 2: Analytical Techniques in Petrochronology

The first part of the day was a recap on chemical mapping techniques used in Petrochronology, especially X-Ray mapping using the scanning electron microscope (SEM) and the electron probe micro analyser (EPMA). A brief explanation on how the electron beams interact with the sample and the physics behind these instruments, as well as detailing both *EDS (energy-dispersive X-Ray spectrometry) and WDS (wavelength-dispersive X-Ray spectrometry) sensors.

day 3: N-Dimensional Compositional Space with CSpace

Another cool round of analytical work using CSpace, a program used to visualize mineral chemistry data in an N-dimensional space. It is possible to project any components onto a ternary or tetrahedral plot, visualize end-member chemistry and plot mineral chemistry analyses. Further reading: Torres-Roldan et al., 2000.

day 4: Compositional Mapping

On Day 4, I finally had the chance to learn new workflows on the new version of XMapTools (Lanari et al., 2014). The current version 4.5 has some cool new features, including a machine-learning-based classification that might save a lot of time while classifying maps and generating mineral masks for further quantification with internal standards.

day 5: Thermodynamic Modelling

During the last day of the first week, we worked with THERIAK-DOMINO, a program by de Capitani & Petrakakis (2010). I used a lot of THERIAK-DOMINO during my master’s and while writing our first paper, but playing around with THERIAK to generate phase volumes versus temperature (using the plotxy program) was pretty fun.

Also, during the week we had a visit from professor Daniel Harlov showing his work with experimental petrology. Several distinct works were presented, showing the strong relation between chemistry of fluids and trace elements, REE zoning due to coupled dissolution and reprecipitation. Some of the references include examples of Pb mobility in alkali- and alkaline-bearing fluids, enabling the resetting of the zircon geochronometer (Harlov et al., 2023); and the case of apatite as a fingerprint for metasomatic processes in different geological contexts (Harlov et al., 2002; Harlov & Foster, 2003; Harlov et al., 2025).
